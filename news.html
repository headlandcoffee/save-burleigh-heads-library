What's happening, in order

This page is the record — replace each placeholder entry with your own campaign updates as they happen, most recent first.

28–29 JULY 2026

Petition tabled, closure delayed, review opened

Cr Josh Martin tabled the 3,000+ signature petition to council. Council confirmed the Burleigh Heads branch — due to close that day — will stay open until the Burleigh Waters refurbishment finishes in September, and opened a formal review of the closure decision. Interim funding was approved to keep a "Library Lounge" running at Burleigh Heads in the meantime. It's a delay and a review, not a reversal.

22 JULY 2026

Petition passes 3,000 signatures in 10 days

Burleigh MP Hermann Vorster's community petition, circulated at local clubs, schools, childcare centres and markets, attracted more than 3,000 signatures in ten days. Organisers pushed back on suggestions that community anger was linked to aggressive behaviour toward library staff.

21 JULY 2026

Signage removed, residents hold final protest

With closure set for 24 July, signage was removed from the front of the building. Hundreds of residents rallied outside the library, including many local schoolkids, as Cr Josh Martin — who voted against the closure — voiced his opposition publicly.

11 JUNE 2026

Closure announced, services cut immediately

Council formally announced the closure and began winding services back straight away: hours cut by 32% (44 to 30 hours a week), Saturday opening scrapped, collections reduced, and the children's literacy program replaced with occasional arts activities elsewhere.

APRIL 2026

Council resolves to close the library

Gold Coast City Council decided, in a closed session during budget deliberations and with no community consultation, to close Burleigh Heads Library permanently and convert the site into a temporary arts and creative hub.

JAN/FEB 2024

Library refurbished under the Master Plan

Burleigh Heads Library was refurbished, consistent with the 2023 Burleigh Heads Business Centre Master Plan's recommendation to develop the site as a community asset — the same site council would vote, just over two years later, to close.
