Community campaign — Burleigh Heads, QLD

One community asset shouldn't have to disappear for another to exist.

Gold Coast City Council planned to permanently close Burleigh Heads Library and turn the building into an arts and creative hub. Locals said no — and we're not done yet.

A reprieve, not a win
The library stays open until the Burleigh Waters refurbishment is complete (expected September) — but with reduced hours, space, books and programming, and council has not reversed its decision to close it permanently. The fight isn't over.

Where things stand

The library was saved for now — not for good

April 2026

Council decides to close the library — behind closed doors

Gold Coast City Council resolved, in a closed session during budget deliberations, to close Burleigh Heads Library and repurpose the building as a temporary arts and creative hub, citing lower patronage than nearby Burleigh Waters Library.

11 June 2026

Decision announced, services cut immediately

Council confirmed the closure and began winding services back straight away: opening hours were cut by 32%, from 44 to 30 hours a week, Saturday opening was scrapped, collections shrank, and the regular children's literacy program was replaced with occasional arts activities at another venue.

21 July 2026

Signage removed, residents rally

With the branch due to close on 24 July, signage was removed from the front of the building — locals called it a "shock move." Councillor Josh Martin, who voted against the closure, joined residents for a final protest outside the library.

22 July 2026

Petition passes 3,000 signatures in 10 days

Burleigh MP Hermann Vorster's community petition, circulated at clubs, schools and markets, attracted more than 3,000 signatures in ten days. Organisers disputed council's suggestion that community anger was linked to aggressive behaviour toward staff.

28–29 July 2026

Petition tabled — council delays closure and opens a review

Cr Josh Martin formally tabled the petition. Council confirmed the branch, due to close that day, would stay open until the Burleigh Waters refurbishment finishes in September, and a review of the closure decision was opened. Interim funding was approved: $20,000 for storing Burleigh Waters' books during its upgrade, and around $80,000 from Crs Martin and Marshall's divisional funds to keep a "Library Lounge" running at Burleigh Heads in the meantime.

Now

The fight for a permanent library isn't over

The library stayed open, but not in the way most locals expected: reduced hours, reduced space, reduced books, reduced programming. A delay and a review are not a reversal, and council has still not released the evidence behind the original decision despite repeated requests — the utilisation analysis, business case, financial assessment and service planning documents it has referred to. We're keeping the pressure on until that evidence is public and the closure is off the table for good, permanently — for a library that turns 60 next year.
